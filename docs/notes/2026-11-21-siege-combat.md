# Siege combat

Roadmap 5.10. A battle at a town with a Fort, Citadel or Castle is fought against its walls. Battles at towns already happen (a garrison, 8.1.6; a hero defending a town, 8.1.11; the computer attacking a town, 8.4.6c), all on an open field until now.

## Parts

Each part is one PR.

1. **5.10.1 Walls and the drawbridge.** A town with a Fort, Citadel or Castle is a siege: the walls stand on the battlefield. Walking stacks can't cross them; flying stacks fly over. The defender's stacks go out and back through the drawbridge, which opens for them and shuts behind them.
2. **5.10.2 Holding the drawbridge.** A dead stack on the drawbridge keeps it open for good, and an attacker on the hex in front of it keeps it shut.
3. **5.10.3 Shooting at the walls.** An attacking shooter outside the walls does half damage to a stack inside them.
4. **5.10.4 Teleport and the walls.** Teleport below Expert can't take a stack across them.
5. **5.10.5 The catapult shoots.** The attacker's hero always has one. It shoots once a round, before every stack, aiming itself: at the wall segment with the fewest hit points left, and once all four are down at the drawbridge. A segment it destroys can be walked through, a destroyed drawbridge stays open for good, and a shot or a Teleport whose line crosses only fallen parts of the wall is no longer stopped by them. The wall segments have 2 hit points, 3 with a Castle; the drawbridge 2.
6. **5.10.6 The catapult as a war machine.** It stands on the battlefield as a target: the defender's stacks and spells can attack it, it never strikes back, and once destroyed it stops shooting. It doesn't count as a stack for winning or losing, gives no experience, and the hero has it again for the next siege.
7. **5.10.7 Earthquake.** The hero casts it from the book with no target: one point of damage to two, three or four random parts of the walls (none or Basic, Advanced, Expert Earth Magic), each picked among the parts still standing, the drawbridge included; "An earthquake will do you no good unless there are town walls to damage." outside a siege.
8. **5.10.8 The moat** (Citadel and Castle): a stack entering it stops there for the round, takes the town's moat damage and has −3 Defense while in it; Advanced Teleport crosses it.
9. **5.10.9 Arrow towers shoot**: the Keep with a Citadel, the upper and lower towers with a Castle; each shoots once a round as the round starts, before the catapult, at a target it picks itself, for its base damage (the Keep 10–20, the others 6–12: the damage of a town with no structures).
9b. **5.10.9b The towers' damage from the town's buildings**: the structures counted as below, 2 more damage each for the Keep, 2 for every two for the other towers.
9c. **5.10.9c The catapult and Earthquake at the towers**: the Keep and the towers have 2 hit points each; the catapult turns to them once the walls and the drawbridge are down; Earthquake's points can land on them; a destroyed tower stops shooting.
10. **5.10.10 Ballistics' shots**: with Ballistics the catapult hits its wall more often and harder (Basic and Advanced 60%, 1 or 2 points; Expert 75%, always 2), shoots twice a round from Advanced, and a second shot whose target fell to the first goes at the nearest part still standing. It still aims itself.
10b. **5.10.10b Aiming the catapult**: the player's hero with Ballistics aims it on a turn of its own, after the towers and before every stack, by clicking a wall segment, the drawbridge, the Keep or a tower, each with its own chance to be hit; the hero can cast a spell on that turn, and Defend passes it.
11. **5.10.11 The computer and the walls**: the walls in a computer hero's weighing of a town (4000 for a Citadel, 10000 for a Castle, 8.4.6c), a threatened town building its walls (8.4.8d), the catapult's automatic aim with Ballistics.

## Rules, as checked

- **Which battles are sieges**: a battle at a town with a Fort, Citadel or Castle. Without one it is an open field, as now. Checked against the wiki (Combat, Siege: "If there is no fortification built in the town, there is a usual battlefield instead") and an open-source engine (walls only when the town has a Fort).
- **Already done**: no retreat or surrender in a town (8.4.6c), and 500 more experience for winning at a town with a Fort or better (8.1.6).
- **Where the walls stand**, checked against the wiki (Wall: "walls fill up 11 hexes, and 6 of them are indestructible", four segments and the drawbridge) and the engine's wall hexes. In our rows and columns (from 0; the engine counts the edge column as its column 0, so ours are one less):

  | Row | Wall | What |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 0 | 11 | the upper tower's base, always blocked |
  | 1 | 11 | Upper Wall |
  | 2, 3 | 10 | indestructible |
  | 4 | 9 | Mid-Upper Wall |
  | 5 | 9 and 10 | the drawbridge: its outer and inner hex |
  | 6 | 9 | indestructible |
  | 7 | 10 | Mid-Lower Wall |
  | 8 | 10 | indestructible |
  | 9 | 11 | indestructible |
  | 10 | 11 | Lower Wall |

  A hex is inside the walls when it is right of its row's wall hex (row 5: right of column 10). The defender's stacks start at column 14, inside. The hex in front of the drawbridge is row 5, column 8.
- **Who can stand where**: no stack stands on a wall hex. Walking stacks can't pass them; flying stacks fly over them, as over any stack (the wiki, Combat: "Flying creatures can fly over walls"; Fortifications: the wall "is impassable by non-flying enemy creatures"). Checked against the engine's battlefield accessibility.
- **The drawbridge**, checked against the wiki (Wall, Drawbridge) and the engine (its gate states and when they change):
  - Shut, its two hexes are walls for the attacker, flyers included; the defender's stacks walk and fly onto and through them, which opens it.
  - It shuts again after an action ends with no stack, living or dead, on either of its hexes. A dead stack on it keeps it open for good, and the attacker can then go through ("drawbridge trapping", the wiki).
  - A stack of the attacker on the hex in front of it (row 5, column 8) keeps it from opening: the defender can't go out through it while it is shut.
  - Its hit points and its destruction come with the catapult (5.10.5): destroyed, it is open for good to both sides, as a dead stack on it makes it, and no stack in front of it keeps anyone off it.
- **Shooting at the walls**: an attacker's shot from outside the walls at a stack behind a standing wall does half damage (the wiki: Wall, "hamper the attacks of ranged attackers by 50%"; Damage, the obstacle penalty R6 = 0.50). The engine halves it when the shooter stands outside the walls and the hexes between it and the target cross a standing wall hex; the drawbridge's outer hex doesn't count. With every wall standing that is any shot from outside at a stack wholly inside. A stack on the drawbridge is not behind the walls (our reading of the engine, where the outer hex doesn't block and the inner one is the line itself). The defender's shots out of the town aren't halved (the engine; the wiki speaks only of targets "behind the wall").
  - **With the distance penalty too**: the wiki's Damage page says the damage "is halved once again if a range penalty applies" (a quarter); the engine halves it only once. We follow the engine (agreed): a shot is halved once, whichever penalties apply.
- **Teleport**: Basic and Advanced can't move a stack from one side of a standing wall to the other; Expert can. Checked against the wiki and the engine (the earlier Teleport note, `2026-11-05-teleport-clone-sacrifice.md`); the engine checks the hexes between the stack and its destination, as for the shooting penalty. The moat's limit comes with 5.10.8. The message is the one an invalid destination already gets ("Invalid Teleport Destination").

- **The catapult**, checked against the wiki (War Machine, Catapult; Fortifications, Fortifications strength and Targeting; Ballistics, its targeting table; Wall) and the engine (its catapult shot, the war machine positions, the battle's wall states):
  - Only the attacking hero has one, and only in a siege. It is a war machine: Attack 10, Defense 10, 1000 health, 24 shots, no damage to creatures, double-wide, doesn't move. On the battlefield it stands on row 7, column 0, its back half on the edge column (the engine's attacker war machine hexes; the attacker's stacks never start on row 7).
  - When: once every round, the first round too, after the arrow towers (5.10.9) and before every stack (the wiki: "the Catapult shoots after the arrow towers, but before any other troop"; the engine puts it in the round's siege phase, first).
  - Hit points: each of the four wall segments has 2, 3 with a Castle; the drawbridge always 2 (the wiki's Fortifications strength table and Wall page; the engine's Fort and Castle fortifications). The Citadel changes nothing here; the Keep and the towers have theirs with 5.10.9.
  - Its aim without Ballistics: the wall segment with the fewest hit points left, one picked at random among the equally damaged; once all four are destroyed, the drawbridge (then the Keep and the towers, 5.10.9). With nothing left to shoot, its turn passes. The wiki and the engine agree word for word.
  - A shot hits its target 50% of the time. A miss hits the nearest standing segment along the wall, counted Upper Wall, Mid-Upper Wall, drawbridge, Mid-Lower Wall, Lower Wall: Upper and Mid-Upper miss onto each other, Mid-Lower and Lower too, the drawbridge onto Mid-Upper or Mid-Lower at even odds; if that one is down, the next nearest; with no other segment standing, it hits its target anyway (the wiki's fixed redirections; the engine's nearest standing segment, at random between equally near ones).
  - Damage: none 10% of the time, 1 point 60%, 2 points 30% (the targeting table; the engine's chances, rolled in that order). A part at 0 is destroyed.
  - A hero with Ballistics: the better chances and the second shot come with 5.10.10, the aim with 5.10.10b.
- **The catapult as a target** (5.10.6), checked against the wiki (War Machine; Template:Creature/Catapult; Combat, Siege; Experience) and the engine (the catapult's creature entry, war machines in battle, the battle's end, its experience):
  - Its stats: Attack 10, Defense 10, 1000 health, one of it, double-wide, speed 0 (the wiki's creature table and the engine agree). It fights for the attacking hero, so the hero's Attack and Defense are added to it as to the hero's creatures (the engine, which ties war machines to the hero's army; the wiki doesn't say; agreed).
  - It never moves and never acts as a stack: without Ballistics it has no turn of its own besides its shot as the round starts (with Ballistics the hero aims it on a turn of its own, 5.10.10), and doesn't count for morale (the wiki: "War Machines are immobile and unaffected by Morale").
  - It never strikes back (the engine: war machines have no retaliation; the wiki says it of the Ballista, and of war machines in their abilities).
  - The defender's stacks attack it as any stack, walking or flying out to it or shooting it. Next to a defender's shooter it keeps that shooter from shooting, as any enemy stack does (the engine counts every adjacent enemy; the wiki says nothing; agreed).
  - Destroyed, it stops shooting for the rest of the battle; the hero has it again for the next siege (the wiki, Combat: "It can be destroyed only temporarily until the end of combat"; the engine keeps the catapult when its stack dies, where other war machines are lost).
  - It doesn't count for winning: an attacker with only the catapult left has lost (the engine leaves war machines out of the battle's end). The winner earns no experience for it (the wiki, Experience: "clones, war machines and bonus health from artifacts do not count"; the engine).
  - Spells: immune to Implosion, Clone, Bloodlust, the mind spells (Berserk, Blind, Forgetfulness, Frenzy, Hypnotize, Mirth, Sorrow), Death Ripple, Destroy Undead, Slow, Haste, Teleport, Resurrection, Sacrifice and Counterstrike; every other spell, damage spells, area spells and Armageddon included, works on it (the wiki's War Machine lists and the engine's spell entries agree, spell for spell). Weakness and Disrupting Ray are in neither of the wiki's lists; the engine lets them through.
  - Creature abilities it is immune to (Death Stare, Aging, Poison and the rest) come with the creatures that have them (6.5).
- **Earthquake** (5.10.7), checked against the wiki (Earthquake; Fortifications, Earthquake; Wall; the game's text `core.genrltxt.182`) and the engine (the spell's config and its catapult effect):
  - A third-level Earth spell, 20 spell points, 17 with Earth Magic (already in `spells.json`). It has no target: cast from the book, it goes off at once.
  - Points: 2 with none or Basic Earth Magic, 3 at Advanced, 4 at Expert. Each point does exactly 1 damage; it never misses and never does none (the wiki: "There is no chance to miss"; the engine: a hit chance of 100, no critical).
  - Where they go: each point at random, at even odds, among the parts that can still take it: the four segments and the drawbridge (later the Keep and the towers, 5.10.9). Several points can land on the same part. All points are picked before any damage is done, and a part already given points enough to destroy it is left out for the rest (the engine; the wiki says the game counts unbuilt towers and wastes points, which we don't, as agreed). With fewer hit points left than points, the rest are lost.
  - Spell power, Sorcery and Resistance don't change it: it touches no stack.
  - Not in a siege (an open field, or a town without a Fort): the book says "An earthquake will do you no good unless there are town walls to damage." (`core.genrltxt.182`, the wiki's copy of the game's texts) and nothing is cast. The engine shows its common "That spell will affect no one!" there instead; we follow the game's own text.
  - Only the attacking side can cast it (the engine; the wiki doesn't say; agreed): the defender's book says "That spell will affect no one!", the engine's text for it, and nothing is cast.
  - With every part already destroyed, the book says the same as outside a siege (the sources don't say; agreed).
- **The moat** (5.10.8), checked against the wiki (Moat; Wall; Fortifications; Combat, Siege; Teleport) and the engine (the Castle town's moat, its obstacle triggers on moving and acting, Teleport's line check):
  - Only with a Citadel or a Castle; a Fort alone has walls and no moat (the wiki's Fortifications and Wall pages; the engine's town fortifications).
  - Where: one hex left of each wall hex, ten hexes: row 0 and 1 column 10, rows 2 and 3 column 9, row 4 column 8, row 6 column 8, rows 7 and 8 column 9, rows 9 and 10 column 10 (the engine's Castle moat hexes, one column less in our numbering). The hex in front of the drawbridge (row 5, column 8) is not moat. The wiki's Wall page says "11 hexes": it counts the drawbridge's outer hex, which crosses the moat, and "creatures crossing it do not suffer moat damage", so the two agree on what a stack feels.
  - Who: both sides' stacks (the wiki: "Both attacking and defending creatures"; the engine's moat has no side). Tower's land mines, which spare the defenders, are another thing (6.5).
  - Stopping: a walking stack that steps into a moat hex stops there, its move over (the wiki: "forced to stop on the moat for the rest of the round"; the engine ends the move on the first moat hex entered). A stack starting its turn in the moat walks out as usual; stepping from one moat hex into another stops it again. A flying stack flies over it and is only touched when it lands in it (the engine checks a flyer's last hex only and never stops it).
  - Defense: −3 while any hex of the stack is in the moat (the wiki; the engine's moat bonus, −3 Defense), never below 0.
  - Damage: 70 for the Castle's moat (the wiki's table; the engine's Castle moat: 70). Other factions' moats (90 for Inferno, Dungeon and Fortress, Fortress two hexes wide, Tower's land mines) come with the town factions (6.5).
    - When: as the stack enters the moat, and then once each round at the start of its turn while it is still in the moat (the game's manual: "a creature troop receives moat damage upon entering the moat and for each turn thereafter the troop remains in the moat at the start of its turn"). The wiki says "when they enter the moat, or end a turn into one"; the engine hits it at the end of each action it takes there, so twice in a round it waits. We follow the manual (agreed): once a round, as its first turn of the round starts, whatever it then does; a stack whose turn comes back after waiting isn't hit again.
    - Not when it is teleported in (the wiki's exception; the manual counts only entering and the start of a turn). The wiki also leaves out a stack that loses its turn (bad morale, Blind, Paralyze). With the manual's timing a stack frozen by bad morale has started its turn, so it is hit (agreed); a blinded or paralysed stack gets no turn at all in our battles, so it isn't, as the wiki says.
    - A walk-and-attack whose way ends in the moat takes the damage before the strike; killed by it, the stack doesn't strike (the engine: the moat stops the move, and a stack killed on the way doesn't attack).
    - The sources name nothing that lessens it: no spell, skill or creature ability (the engine casts it as a nameless, non-magical damage effect). The game has no log text for it; ours is our own wording.
  - Teleport: below Advanced it can't take a stack across the moat (the wiki: Basic "cannot teleport over walls or moats", Advanced "can teleport over moats (but not walls)"). The engine checks it as it checks the walls: only between the two sides of the walls, when a moat hex is on the way between the stack and its destination, the destination itself left out. With the walls standing, the walls already refuse those below Expert, so the moat only matters through a gap: Basic is refused there, Advanced goes through. Teleporting a stack into the moat is allowed and does it no damage.
  - Shots are not changed by the moat (the engine checks only the walls for the shooting penalty).
- **The arrow towers** (5.10.9), checked against the wiki (Arrow tower; Fortifications; Combat, the round's order; Wall) and the engine (its arrow tower creature, the tower damage script, the battle's setup and its round's siege phase, the automatic shooter's target choice); the texts against the wiki's copy of the game's texts:
  - Which: the Keep (the wiki's "primary arrow tower") with a Citadel or a Castle; the upper and lower towers (the "secondary" ones) with a Castle only. A Fort has none. Both sources agree. They are there whoever defends the town: a hero, the garrison alone, or both.
  - Where: none of them stands on a hex a stack can use. The engine puts them off the battlefield, and aims at them on its hexes for row 2's right edge (the Keep), row 0, column 11 (the upper tower) and row 10, column 12 (the lower tower). We draw them where the game's picture shows them (agreed): the Keep at the back in the middle, on row 5's right edge (column 15, the edge column), and the upper and lower towers just past the wall's two ends, off the battlefield: above row 0, column 11 and below row 10, column 11. In the data a tower's `hex` is the hex it is drawn by and `stands` (`above` or `below`) puts it past that hex.
  - Damage, the same in the wiki's formula and the engine's script: the Keep does 10 + 2 × N to twice that, the upper and lower towers 6 + 2 × ⌊N / 2⌋ to twice that, N being the town's "structures". The roll is the damage: the tower is one creature with Attack 0, the target's Defense is ignored, there is no distance or walls penalty, no luck, and the defending hero's skills (Archery, Attack) don't add to it. The wiki's table for Castle (17 structures: 44–88 and 22–44) matches.
  - Structures (the wiki's list; the engine counts the Town Hall in the Village Hall's place, leaves out the Fort's chain and counts only buildings that upgrade nothing): the Town Hall (once, whatever hall above it), the Tavern, Marketplace, Resource Silo, Blacksmith, Mage Guild (once, whatever its level), Shipyard, the Grail building, each of the seven dwellings (once, upgraded or not), and the faction's own: for Castle the Lighthouse and the Stables, not the Brotherhood of the Sword or the Griffin Bastion. The Village Hall, the Fort, Citadel and Castle don't count.
  - When: once every round, the first round too, before the catapult and before every stack: the Keep, then the upper tower, then the lower one (the wiki, Combat: "the round starts with the Arrow Towers (main, then top, then bottom), and the Catapult"; the engine's siege phase, towers before the catapult, in that order).
  - Target, chosen by the computer whoever defends (without Artillery, which comes later): any living stack of the attacker's side, wherever it stands. The engine weighs them in this order: a stack that can act before one that can't (blinded, paralysed); a creature before a war machine (the catapult); one that can hurt the defenders this turn or the next (a shooter with shots, or a stack that can reach a defender's stack by its next turn) before one that can't; one inside the walls before one outside; then the most worth taken (the average damage, at most the stack's health, times its worth per health point, plus the average kills times its worth per creature, by `aiValue`). The wiki gives classes by when the stack can attack (now, next turn, later) and a priority of worth over health with the damage counted as 1 (a bug), ties to the army's rightmost slot. The two agree on putting the stacks that can strike soon first; they differ in the weighing after that. We follow the engine's order, a tie going to the first stack in the attacker's army (agreed).
  - Texts: the log line is the game's for any attack, with the creature's name: "The Arrow Tower does %d damage." (`core.genrltxt.376`, the name from the wiki's copy of `CRTRAITS.TXT`: Arrow Tower, Arrow Towers), then the losses as for any damage ("3 Pikemen perish.").
  - It doesn't count as a stack for winning: with every stack of the defender dead the attacker wins, the towers standing or not (the engine leaves the towers out of the battle's end). No experience for it.
  - Air Shield: the wiki says that in the original game it raises the towers' damage instead of lowering it (a bug, fixed in Horn of the Abyss); the engine lowers it as for any shot. We lower it as for any shot (agreed).
- **The Keep and the towers as targets** (5.10.9c), checked against the wiki (Fortifications: its strength table and Targeting; the Targeting table on Ballistics; Earthquake; Combat, Siege) and the engine (the town's fortification hit points, the catapult's target choice and miss, Earthquake's point lottery, a destroyed tower's shooter, the tower's battlement drawn only while it stands):
  - Hit points: the Keep 2 (Citadel and Castle), the upper and lower towers 2 each (Castle). The two sources agree.
  - The catapult's aim without Ballistics: the four segments, then the drawbridge, then the Keep, then the upper and lower towers. The wiki says only "then to the secondary towers"; the engine picks one of the two at random, then the other.
  - Hitting: the wiki's table gives the Keep 5% and the towers 10% without Ballistics, but only "before all walls are destroyed", and a miss hits the nearest standing wall segment or, with none left, the target anyway (the wiki: Keep → Lower Wall, upper tower → Upper Wall, lower tower → Lower Wall; the engine the same). Without Ballistics the catapult turns to the towers only once every segment is down, so its shot at a tower always lands on it. The chances matter only with an aimed catapult (5.10.10).
  - Damage: as for any catapult shot, none 10%, 1 point 60%, 2 points 30%.
  - Earthquake: the Keep and the towers are among the parts its points can land on, at even odds with the rest (both sources). The wiki counts towers the town hasn't built and wastes points on them; the engine only counts built ones. We already follow the engine there (5.10.7, agreed). The wiki gives the order it goes through the parts: the upper tower, the four segments and the drawbridge from the top, the lower tower, the Keep last.
  - A destroyed tower: its shooter is gone for the rest of the battle (the engine removes the tower's stack as the shot lands; the wiki, Earthquake's bug, speaks of "an unguarded arrow tower"), and its battlement is no longer drawn. A damaged tower shoots as before, for its full damage (the engine; the wiki says nothing of less damage).
  - Texts: the game logs no catapult hits (5.10.5); the tower's popup says "The %s is destroyed." (`core.genrltxt.154`) once it falls, which comes with the towers' popup.
- **Ballistics** (5.10.10, 5.10.10b), checked against the wiki (Ballistics, its skill texts for the original game and the Targeting table, read without its Horn of the Abyss values; Fortifications, Targeting: aimed and automatic shots; Combat: the siege's order of turns; `SSTRAITS.TXT` for the skill's texts) and the engine (its catapult shot's levels, its target choice, miss and second shot, and which units a hero controls):
  - Shots a round: 1 with none or Basic, 2 at Advanced and Expert. Both go at the same target; if the first destroys it, the second goes at the nearest part still standing, of any kind (a tower included), one at random among equally near ones; with nothing left it isn't fired. The engine counts the parts along the wall from the top: the upper tower 0, the Upper Wall 1, the Mid-Upper Wall 2, the drawbridge 3, the Mid-Lower Wall 4, the Lower Wall 5, and the lower tower and the Keep both 6 (so from the Lower Wall the Mid-Lower Wall, the lower tower and the Keep are equally near). The wiki says only "the nearest target that still stands, of any type".
  - The chance to hit the part aimed at, by level (none, Basic, Advanced, Expert): a wall segment 50, 60, 60, 75%; the drawbridge 25, 30, 30, 40%; the Keep 5, 7, 7, 10%; the upper and lower towers 10, 15, 15, 20%. The wiki's table and the engine agree, number for number. (The wiki's "100% at Expert" and "always hits the intended target" are Horn of the Abyss's; the original game's text says "two aimed shots at maximum damage".) A miss lands as before: on the nearest standing segment (the Keep onto the Lower Wall, the upper tower onto the Upper Wall, the lower tower onto the Lower Wall), or on the target itself when no other segment stands.
  - Damage, by level: none 10, 0, 0, 0%; 1 point 60, 50, 50, 0%; 2 points 30, 50, 50, 100%. The two sources agree.
  - Who aims: the hero with Basic Ballistics or better aims it, clicking a standing wall segment, the drawbridge, the Keep or a tower; destroyed parts can't be picked (the wiki, Aimed shots; the engine gives the hero's side control of the catapult with any level of the skill). It aims itself when the hero has no Ballistics, during auto combat and for a computer player. A catapult aiming itself with Ballistics goes for the drawbridge first, then the walls while all four stand, then the Keep and the towers (the wiki and the engine agree); that is 5.10.11 (agreed in the plan): until then it aims as without the skill, with the skill's chances and shots.
  - Its turn: after the arrow towers, before every stack, as its shot now (the wiki, Combat). The wiki's Combat page: a controlled catapult "can be set to wait (starts at beginning of waiting phase), while defense provides no bonuses". The hero can cast a spell on it (the wiki's Ballistics page: "it gives you first chance to cast spells in sieges"; the engine lets the hero cast on any turn of a unit the player controls), and the catapult then still shoots, as a stack still acts after the hero's spell.
  - The skill's texts, the game's own (`SSTRAITS.TXT`, the same words as the wiki's skill page for the original game): "Basic Ballistics gives control of the catapult to the hero, allowing aimed shots with increased damage.", "Advanced Ballistics gives control of the catapult to the hero, allowing two aimed shots with increased damage.", "Expert Ballistics gives control of the catapult to the hero, allowing two aimed shots at maximum damage."
  - Not found in either source: a status bar text over a wall part on the catapult's turn (the engine shows only its catapult cursor).
- **A destroyed wall segment**: its hex can be walked on and through by both sides (the wiki, Wall: "hero forces can move onto destructible wall segments after destroying them"; the engine's battlefield accessibility). The six indestructible hexes and the upper tower's base stay blocked.
- **Shots and Teleport through a broken wall**: the engine walks the shortest way of hexes from the shooter's hex (the stack's own for Teleport) to the target's hex, each step to the neighbour nearest the target, a tie to the one further along towards it, then nearer its row; the shot is halved (Teleport below Expert refused) when the two hexes are on different sides of the walls and a hex on that way is a standing wall part. The engine leaves the end hex out and counts the drawbridge's inner hex as inside; we count it outside (5.10.4), so we keep the end hex in, which keeps a stack inside from being teleported onto the standing drawbridge below Expert, as agreed in 5.10.4. The indestructible hexes always count, the four segments and the drawbridge's inner hex while they stand (open or shut), the drawbridge's outer hex never. So with every part standing nothing changes, and once a segment is down a shot or a Teleport going through its gap is not stopped (the wiki says only "behind the wall"; the engine's line check, the same for shots and Teleport).
## What we decided

- **The battlefield**: the town gate's terrain, as for the open-field battles at towns, with the walls drawn on their hexes and the drawbridge drawn shut or open. The game shows the town's own siege picture instead; that waits for pictures (10.2).
- **The combat AI** (agreed): unchanged, with the walls in its paths. A computer defender's stack goes out through the drawbridge to its nearest target; a computer attacker's walker that can't reach any target passes its turn, as now. The game's AI tends to stay inside; that is smarter combat AI (5.7b).
- **Before the catapult (5.10.5)**, an attacker with only walkers couldn't get in unless the defenders came out (agreed); since 5.10.2 also when a stack died on the drawbridge. Now the catapult opens a way within a few rounds.
- **What is saved**: nothing new. Whether the battle is a siege follows from the town's buildings when the battle started, and the drawbridge from where the stacks are: it is open while a living stack covers one of its hexes.
- **5.10.2, holding the drawbridge**: open while any stack, living or dead, covers one of its hexes; a sacrificed stack leaves no corpse, so it doesn't. It stays worked out from where the stacks are, so nothing new is saved. While it is shut, a living stack of the attacker on the hex in front of it keeps the defender's stacks off its two hexes; a corpse there doesn't (the sources don't say; our call). The defender's flyers still fly out over the walls.
- **Inside the walls** (5.10.3, 5.10.4): a hex is inside when it is right of its row's wall hex; the drawbridge's two hexes are outside. A shot is halved for the walls when the shooter stands outside and every hex of the target is inside, whichever side either fights for (the engine goes by where they stand). The shot's cursor and damage range show it as they show the distance penalty, since the game has one cursor for either penalty. A two-hex stack with one hex on the drawbridge is not wholly inside, so a shot at it is not halved (agreed).
- **The computer's shooters** (5.10.3): they still shoot the nearest stack, so the halving doesn't change which one they pick; weighing the damage is smarter combat AI (5.7b). A computer attacker's shooter starts more than 10 hexes from every hex inside the walls, so until something brings it closer its shots into the town are halved for the distance anyway.
- **5.10.4, Teleport**: below Expert, the stack and the hex it is taken to must both be inside or both outside; otherwise the hex says "Invalid Teleport Destination" and clicking it does nothing.
  - Which hexes count: the stack's own hex (its front hex for a double-wide stack) and the hex it is taken to, as the engine checks the line between those two. The drawbridge's hexes are outside, so below Expert a defender's stack inside can't be taken onto the drawbridge (the engine agrees: the drawbridge's inner hex blocks the line while it stands).
  - Who: whichever side the player's hero fights for, attacking a town or defending one. The computer doesn't cast Teleport yet.
  - Data: Teleport's `teleports` becomes `{ "acrossWalls": [false, false, false, true] }`, by skill level (none, Basic, Advanced, Expert); the moat's `acrossMoat` joins it in 5.10.8. Nothing new is saved.
  - Since the catapult brings segments down (5.10.5), the check is whether the way between the two hexes crosses a standing part of the walls, as for the shooting penalty.
- **5.10.5, the catapult shooting**:
  - Who has one: the attacking hero in every siege, the player's or a computer player's; nothing is bought or carried for it yet (war machines in the hero's slots, 7.8 and the Blacksmith, 6.2, come later).
  - It is drawn on its two hexes (agreed), which no stack can enter, but it isn't a stack yet: not in the turn order, not a target, no right-click popup. That is 5.10.6.
  - Its shot comes as the round starts, before the first stack's turn: in the first round as the battle opens, in each later one as the last stack of the round ends its turn. A reload part way through a round doesn't shoot again. The stone flies for four battle steps (0.7 seconds at the default speed); no stack acts until it lands.
  - How it is shown (agreed): a stone flies from the catapult to the part it hits; only when it lands does the part change its picture (standing, damaged, destroyed), with a puff of dust where it hit, cracks for damage and falling rubble for a destroyed part, and nothing but the dust when it does no damage. The combat log says what happened, in our own wording since the game logs nothing (agreed): "The Catapult hits the Upper Wall.", "The Catapult hits the Upper Wall, but does no damage.", "The Catapult destroys the Upper Wall." (the part names are the wiki's: Upper Wall, Mid-Upper Wall, Mid-Lower Wall, Lower Wall, Drawbridge).
  - Damaged means it has lost any hit points: a Castle's wall at 2 of 3 is damaged too (agreed; the game has a picture for each state but the sources don't say which a Castle's wall at 2 shows).
  - What is saved: each shot, as one fact with the part it hit and the damage, so the walls are rebuilt on reload as they fell. The town's Castle at the start of the battle gives the hit points.
  - Inside the walls stays as it is (right of the row's wall hex); a destroyed segment's own hex is outside, like the drawbridge's.
  - The way from shooter to target (and from the stack to its Teleport destination) is walked as the engine does; the target's hex is its own hex, the front one for a double-wide stack.
  - The computer: unchanged, its walkers find the way through a gap or the fallen drawbridge as they find any other way.
- **5.10.6, the catapult as a target**:
  - It becomes a stack of the attacker's side, on the two hexes it already stands on, with its own id so the attacks and spells already saved name it. Nothing new is saved: its damage is rebuilt from the attacks and spells that hit it, and whether the battle has it from the town's buildings.
  - It is drawn with its own picture as now, with its count, as every stack; destroyed, as a wreck that, like a corpse, no longer blocks its hexes.
  - The cursor, the status bar and the damage range over it are those of any enemy stack ("Attack Catapult (…)"); the combat log says the damage as for any stack and, once it is destroyed, "The Catapult is destroyed." in place of "One Catapult perishes." (agreed; the game's text `core.genrltxt.154`, "The %s is destroyed.", which the engine shows for an arrow tower; the engine logs a destroyed catapult as any stack that perishes).
  - It is in the attacker's casualties on the results window when it was destroyed (the engine lists war machines among the losses; the wiki says nothing; agreed), and is not taken from the hero's army.
  - The computer's stacks go for the nearest enemy stack as now, the catapult included; the attacker's own stacks and spells treat it as one of theirs (Cure, Shield and the like can be cast on it).
  - Spells: the ones it is immune to get `notOnWarMachines` in `spells.json`, and the mind spells skip it as they skip the undead.
- **5.10.7, Earthquake**:
  - What is saved: the cast, as the spell's fact with the parts it hit and the damage each took, so a reload rebuilds the walls as it left them and rolls nothing again. Whether it can be cast follows from the walls.
  - How it is shown: no stone; each part hit gets the dust and the cracks or rubble a catapult hit shows, all at once as the cast lands. The combat log says "<Hero> casts 'Earthquake'." as for any spell, then a line for each part, in our own wording since the sources give none: "The Earthquake damages the Upper Wall." or "The Earthquake destroys the Upper Wall.", in the walls' order from the top.
  - The catapult then aims as before: a segment the Earthquake damaged is the weakest, so it goes for that one next.
  - The computer doesn't cast spells yet, so only the player's hero casts it (5.10.11 and 5.7b for the computer).
- **5.10.8, the moat**:
  - Whether the battle has it follows from the town's buildings when the battle started (a Citadel or a Castle), as the walls do. Nothing new is saved: its damage is worked out again from the moves and actions already saved.
  - How it is shown: water drawn on its ten hexes under the stacks. A stack taking its damage is shown as any stack hit by a spell, and the log says "The Moat does 70 damage." and the losses, as for any damage ("One Pikeman perishes."), in our own wording.
  - Reach: the hexes a walking stack can reach are those it can get to without passing through a moat hex; it can end its move on one.
  - The town's Fort icon (the town box in the info box and the right-click popup, and the town screen's Fort button) shows water under the walls for a Citadel and a Castle, as the game's icon shows the moat.
  - Teleport's data: `teleports` gets `"acrossMoat": [false, false, true, true]`, by skill level.
  - The computer: its walkers head for their target as now, the way through the moat counting as any other way, and stop in the moat on it; they don't steer around it (smarter combat AI, 5.7b).
- **5.10.8b, the moat from the town's data** (a refactor before 5.10.9; nothing the player sees changes):
  - Everything the moat has today, its numbers and its behaviour, comes from its town type's entry in `game/data/towns.json`, so another town's moat made of the same parts is a data entry, no code (agreed: the behaviour too, not only the numbers):
    ```json
    "moat": {
      "name": "Moat",
      "from": "CITADEL",
      "damage": 70,
      "defensePenalty": 3,
      "stopsWalkers": true,
      "hitsOnEntering": true,
      "hitsAsTurnStarts": true,
      "affects": ["attacker", "defender"],
      "hexes": [{ "row": 0, "column": 10 }, "…the ten hexes"]
    }
    ```
    - `from`: the lowest fort that has it, counted up the Fort, Citadel, Castle chain (a Castle has a Citadel's moat). A town type without `moat` has walls and no moat, whatever its fort.
    - `hexes`: listed one by one in the battle's rows and columns (from 0), so a two-hex-wide moat or any other shape is only a longer list.
    - `name`: the log line ("The Moat does 70 damage."), the hit's effect and the hex's label on the battlefield.
    - `stopsWalkers`: a walking stack stops on the first moat hex it steps into, and can't reach what lies past the moat that turn. Without it, walkers walk through.
    - `hitsOnEntering` and `hitsAsTurnStarts`: the damage as a stack goes in (a flyer as it lands), and once a round as the turn of a stack still in it starts.
    - `affects`: the sides it works on; a stack of a side left out isn't stopped, hit or weakened by it.
  - What every moat still does in code: a stack teleported in isn't hit, a stack whose turn comes back after waiting isn't hit again, and Teleport's `acrossMoat` decides whether Teleport crosses it, whichever side the moat affects. A moat that does something none has yet (Tower's land mines, each going off once) adds its own keys and code with it (6.5).
  - Nothing new is saved: the moat comes from the town's type and buildings when the battle started, as the walls do.
  - Left as it is: the Fort icon's water, drawn by the fort's level (the town's pictures, 10.2).
- **5.10.9, the arrow towers shooting**:
  - Where the numbers live: the town type's `arrowTowers` in `towns.json`, as the moat's do: for each tower the fort it comes with and its base damage (5.10.9); the damage per structure and whether the tower gets half of the count come with 5.10.9b, where a building that isn't a structure though it upgrades nothing says so in its data (Castle's Brotherhood of the Sword and Griffin Bastion).
  - Damage in 5.10.9: the base damage alone, a roll from it to twice it (the Keep 10–20, the others 6–12), whatever the town has built; Air Shield lowers it as for any shot.
  - The towers aren't stacks: they belong to the walls, aren't in the turn order, can't be attacked, walked to or cast on, and the battle's end and experience don't see them.
  - What is saved: each tower's shot as one fact with the tower, its target and the damage rolled, so a reload rebuilds the losses and rolls nothing again. Which towers the battle has follows from the town's buildings when it started; their damage from the buildings then too.
  - How it is shown: each tower drawn where it stands (the Keep at the back on row 5's right edge, the upper and lower towers above and below the wall's two ends, agreed), an arrow flying from the tower to its target for four battle steps, as the catapult's stone does, then the target shown hit as by any shot; the log says "The Arrow Tower does 52 damage." and the losses. The next tower, then the catapult, shoot once it has landed.
  - A reload part way through a round doesn't shoot again, as for the catapult.
- **5.10.9b, the towers' damage from the town's buildings** (rules checked again against the wiki's Arrow tower page and the engine's tower damage script and its building count; they agree):
  - Each tower in `arrowTowers` gets `perStructure`, the damage one structure adds as a range (`{ "min": 2, "max": 4 }`, the wiki's "d = 2..4"; the engine's +2 to the least with the most twice the least comes to the same), and the upper and lower towers `halfTheStructures: true`, the count halved and rounded down. The roll then goes from `damage.min + perStructure.min × N` to `damage.max + perStructure.max × N`.
  - Which buildings are structures: one that upgrades no other building (its `upgrades` in the data), unless its data says otherwise with `structure`: `false` on the Village Hall and the Fort (they upgrade nothing but don't count), `true` on the Town Hall (it upgrades the Village Hall but counts), `false` on Castle's Brotherhood of the Sword and Griffin Bastion (in the game they upgrade the Tavern and the Griffin Tower; our data has them only require them). So a dwelling and its upgrade count once, the Mage Guild once whatever its level, the Town Hall once whatever hall stands above it, and the Citadel and Castle not at all. The town's built buildings already hold every step of a chain.
  - Castle's own count, as the wiki's table: the Town Hall, Tavern, Marketplace, Resource Silo, Blacksmith, Mage Guild, Shipyard, Lighthouse, Stables and the seven dwellings, 16 until the Grail building comes (with the Grail) and 17 with it, the wiki's count: the Keep 42–84 and the others 22–44, then 44–88 and 22–44.
  - The damage is worked out from the buildings the town had when the battle started, with the towers; each shot saves the damage it did, so nothing new is saved.
  - Left for later: the towers' popup showing their damage (with the battle's stack popups); the Grail building as a structure (with the Grail).
- **5.10.9c, the catapult and Earthquake at the towers**:
  - Each tower in `arrowTowers` gets `hitPoints: 2` in `towns.json`, so the walls' parts are the four segments, the drawbridge and the towers the town has built.
  - The catapult's aim (agreed): the Keep once the segments and the drawbridge are down, then the upper or the lower tower at random, then the other; with every part down its turn passes, as now.
  - A tower has two pictures, standing and destroyed (the engine draws no damaged tower, where a wall segment has one): a damaged tower looks as it did, a destroyed one is a ruin without its battlements, each with the dust and rubble of the hit on it; its label says "Keep", "Damaged Keep" or "Destroyed Keep".
  - Log lines, our own wording as for the walls: "The Catapult hits the Keep.", "The Catapult hits the Keep, but does no damage.", "The Catapult destroys the Keep.", "The Earthquake damages the Upper Tower.", "The Earthquake destroys the Upper Tower.". Earthquake's lines come in the wiki's order: the upper tower, the walls from the top, the lower tower, the Keep.
  - A destroyed tower doesn't shoot in later rounds. One destroyed by Earthquake after it shot this round has done its shooting; the next round it is passed over.
  - Earthquake can be cast while any part, a tower included, still stands; with every part down the book says "An earthquake will do you no good unless there are town walls to damage." as now.
  - What is saved: nothing new; the catapult's shot and Earthquake's fact already name the part hit, now a tower too.
  - Left for later: the towers' own hit chances and the aimed catapult (5.10.10); the towers' popup with "The %s is destroyed." (with the battle's stack popups).
- **5.10.10, Ballistics' shots** (agreed):
  - The chances, damage and shots per level live in the catapult's data (`siegeShots` on `CATAPULT` in `monsters.json`), by skill level (none, Basic, Advanced, Expert), with the skill that sets the level; each arrow tower's `aimedAs` in `towns.json` says which chance it is hit with (`keep` or `tower`).
  - Any attacking hero with the skill gets them, the player's or a computer player's; the catapult still aims itself as without the skill until 5.10.10b (the player's aim) and 5.10.11 (the computer's).
  - The second shot: a second stone flies once the first has landed, and the log has a line for each. Each shot is saved as its stone sets off, so a reload while the second flies shows both shots and fires neither again.
  - What is saved: each shot as now, its part and damage, and now also the part it was aimed at, which the second shot goes at; how many it has fired this round follows from them. A shot saved before this part has no aim and counts as aimed at the part it hit.
  - The skill's popup on the hero screen says the game's texts above, in place of our own words.
- **5.10.10b, aiming the catapult** (agreed):
  - On its turn the catapult is the active stack. Over a standing segment, the drawbridge (either hex), the Keep or a tower's picture the cursor is the catapult's; over anything else it is the blocked cursor and a click does nothing. A click fires its shot or shots at that part.
  - The status bar over a part, in our own wording since the sources give none: "Shoot the Catapult at the Upper Wall".
  - The hero can cast a spell first; the catapult's turn goes on after it. Defend passes the catapult's turn with no bonus. Wait is greyed out on its turn (left for later). Auto combat lets it aim itself.
  - What is saved: the shots as now. A reload on the catapult's turn is still the catapult's turn, after the first of two shots too.
- **How it looks**: stone hexes for the walls, and wooden planks for the drawbridge, lowered while it is open; from 5.10.5 cracked stone for a damaged segment and rubble for a destroyed one, broken planks for a destroyed drawbridge. A wall hex is out of reach like any other; the game's status bar text over the walls waits for the siege's picture (10.2).
