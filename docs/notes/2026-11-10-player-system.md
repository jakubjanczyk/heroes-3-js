# Player system

Roadmap 8.1. Other players on the map, whose turn it is, and what a player can learn about the others. The AI that plays them is 8.3 and 8.4, hot-seat is 8.2.

## Parts

Each part is one PR.

Other players on the map:

1. **8.1.1 Other players' colours.** Heroes, towns, mines and dwellings of other colours carry their owner's flag (every hero gets a flag, the player's own too), show in that colour on the minimap and say their owner in the popup ("(Blue)"). The kingdom overview shows the other players' flags under Enemies.
2. **8.1.2 Enemy heroes stand in the way.** The player can't select an enemy hero or meet it, and it blocks its tile. The swords show over one the hero can reach, and the hero walks up to it and stops next to it; the battle comes with 8.1.7.
3. **8.1.3 What an enemy hero's popup shows.** Its name and army, each stack's size as a word. No primary skills, morale, luck or spell points.
4. **8.1.4 Capturing enemy mines and dwellings.** Flagging one makes it the player's: the flag turns red, and the income or the recruits go to the player.
5. **8.1.5 Capturing enemy towns.** Walking into an enemy town without defenders captures it: red flag, the town list, its income.

Fighting for towns and heroes:

6. **8.1.6 Attacking a town's garrison.** Entering an enemy town with a garrison and no hero starts a battle against the garrison; winning captures the town, losing leaves the town and what is left of its garrison as they were. A neutral town given a garrison by the scenario is fought the same way (6.4). Until sieges (5.10) every such battle is fought on an open field, walls or not.
7. **8.1.7 Attacking an enemy hero.** The swords cursor, then a battle against the hero's army, with its primary skills. The winner gets the experience and the beaten enemy hero leaves the map.
7b. **8.1.7b An enemy hero's Resistance.** The skill's chance to block the player's spells on the enemy hero's army (4.8.6). Its rules are checked when it is planned.
8. **8.1.8 The beaten hero's artifacts go to the winner.** Every worn and carried artifact moves to the player's hero, as picking them up would, and a message names them; what finds no room is lost.
9. **8.1.9 Losing to an enemy hero.** The player's beaten hero leaves for the tavern (4.5.5), the enemy hero gets the experience, levels up on its own, and takes the beaten hero's artifacts; after a retreat it gets only the experience for the creatures killed.
10. **8.1.10 Surrender** to an enemy hero: pay half the recruitment cost of the creatures left, in gold.
11. **8.1.11 An enemy hero defends its town.** Entering a town with an enemy hero in it starts a battle against that hero, and the town is captured if the player wins.

Turns:

12. **8.1.12 Taking turns.** After End Turn each other player takes a turn in colour order, then the new day starts. The turns are empty until the AI plays them (8.3).
13. **8.1.13 Each player's day.** Each player's heroes get their movement points back, and each player gets their own income and starting resources.
14. **8.1.14 The hourglass.** The info box shows an hourglass with the player's crest during another player's turn.

What a player learns about the others:

15. **8.1.15 The town box for towns that aren't yours.** No hall or income, and army sizes as words.
16. **8.1.16 Visions on enemy heroes** (Advanced) **and towns** (Expert).
17. **8.1.17 The Thieves' Guild.**

Later: Disguise and enemy heroes casting in battle (Eagle Eye, Magic Mirror, Cure, Resistance) come with the AI (8.3, 5.7b), and losing the game with victory conditions (8.5). The random starting garrison of a neutral town the scenario gives none, and its weekly reinforcements, come with neutral towns' garrisons (6.4).

## Rules, as checked

- **Colours**, in turn order: red, blue, tan, green, orange, purple, teal, pink. Checked against the wiki's copy of `ARRAYTXT.TXT` (texts 23 to 30, "Owned by red player" and so on) and an open-source engine's player colours.
- **Enemy hero popup** (right-click): the hero's name and army, each stack's size as a word and not a number. No primary skills, morale, luck or spell points. Checked against an open-source engine's hero info (the basic level for a hero of another player) and the game manual, which says Visions at Advanced shows an enemy hero's primary skills and the "composition and quantity" of its army, so they're hidden without it.
- **Army size words** in an army slot: Few, Several, Pack, Lots, Horde, Throng, Swarm, Zounds, Legion (`ARRAYTXT.TXT` 174 to 198, every third). The bands are the monsters' bands (`game/heroes/monster-count.js`). Checked against the wiki's `ARRAYTXT.TXT` and the engine's army tooltip.
- **The enemy hero popup's box** is the hero box the player's own heroes get, with the portrait, the name and the seven army slots. The primary skill values, morale, luck and spell points are left empty; each slot shows the creature with its size word under it in place of the count. Checked again for 8.1.3 against an open-source engine's hero tooltip (the four skills, spell points, morale and luck are drawn only for a detailed hero, the slot's word is `ARRAYTXT.TXT` 171 + 3 × the band, counting Few as 1) and the wiki's `ARRAYTXT.TXT` (Few at 174 through Legion at 198).
- **The size words' bands** are the monsters' bands: 1 to 4 Few, 5 Several, 10 Pack, 20 Lots, 50 Horde, 100 Throng, 250 Swarm, 500 Zounds, 1000 Legion. Checked against the engine's quantity bands and `game/heroes/monster-count.js`.
- **Status bar** over another player's hero: the engine shows "%s the %s" (`GENRLTXT.TXT` 15) and the owner line. Our status bar names heroes by their name alone. We keep that for every hero: the class isn't shown anywhere on the map yet.
- **Cursor**: swords over an enemy hero the hero can reach, which is the attack (8.1.7), numbered with the day like any other target; the arrow over one it can't reach.
- **Capturing**: entering a town of another player that has no defenders captures it, like a neutral one. A town with a garrison and no hero is defended by the garrison. With a Fort, Citadel or Castle that is a siege; without one, an ordinary battle on an open field (the wiki, Town). A hero in the town defends it with their own army, and in a siege the garrison's stacks join them (the engine), checked again in 8.1.11. Flagging a mine or dwelling of another player makes it the visitor's. Checked against the wiki (Town, Mine) and the engine.
- **Capturing mines and dwellings** (checked again for 8.1.4 against the wiki's Mine and Creature dwelling pages and an open-source engine's mine and dwelling visits): entering a mine or dwelling another player owns flags it for the visitor, exactly as a neutral one: the same mine message with its daily income, and a dwelling then offers its creatures. A mine pays its new owner from the next day; the old owner's income loses it at once. A flagged dwelling's +1 growth moves to the new owner's towns from the next week.
- **Guarded dwellings of another player**: the level 5 to 7 dwellings' guards come back at the start of every week whoever owns the dwelling, and guard it against every player but its owner, a pre-flagged one included. So a hero fights the full guards of another player's Monastery (same question, `core.genrltxt.421`); winning flags it, and for the rest of that week it can be re-flagged without a fight ("may be re-flagged peacefully by enemy heroes any number of times during that week", the wiki).
- **Guards an owner leaves in a mine** ("Unfortunately, it appears that the owner has left some guards. Do you wish to fight them?", the wiki's Mine page) need garrisons in mines, which nothing builds yet: left out.
- **Attacking a town's garrison** (checked for 8.1.6 against the wiki's Town and Experience pages, its `GENRLTXT.TXT`, and an open-source engine's town visit, pathfinding, battle results and results window):
  - A town of another player, or a neutral town, with creatures in its garrison and no hero is fought by entering it: the hero walks onto the town's gate and the battle starts straight away, with no question first. The cursor over it is the swords, not the horse; a town with an empty garrison keeps the horse and is taken without a fight (8.1.5).
  - The garrison fights as it stands, each stack in its own slot, with no hero: no spells, primary skills, morale or luck from a hero on its side.
  - Winning empties the garrison and the town becomes the player's: its flag, the town list, its income, the fog around it. The hero stays in the town. The engine doesn't open the town screen after the battle; we open it, as entering an undefended town does (agreed for 8.1.6).
  - Experience is one point per hit point of the creatures killed, as against monsters. The wiki gives 500 more for "an enemy town captured during a siege"; the engine gives those 500 for every won battle at a town, with walls or without. We follow the wiki (agreed for 8.1.6): the 500 come only for a town with a Fort, Citadel or Castle, where the battle would be a siege.
  - Losing or retreating takes the hero off the map and leaves the town to its owner with what is left of the garrison. Surrender needs an enemy hero, so it's disabled; its tooltip, "There is no hero to surrender to", is our own wording.
  - The results window names the defender by its strongest creature (the highest AI value), as for monsters; the texts are the ones the battles already use.
- **Attacking an enemy hero** (checked for 8.1.7 against the wiki's Experience page and an open-source engine's battle setup and battle results):
  - The swords over an enemy hero the hero can reach (8.1.2); the hero walks up next to it and the battle starts straight away, with no question first, on the enemy hero's tile's terrain.
  - The enemy hero fights with its whole army, each stack in its own slot, and its stacks get what a hero gives: its Attack and Defense (artifacts included), its army's morale and its luck (Leadership, Luck, artifacts). An artifact that cancels morale or luck in battle does so for both sides whichever hero wears it, and an artifact's spell immunities protect its wearer's army only.
  - The hero stands on the right of the battlefield, the player's on the left.
  - Winning: one experience point per hit point of the enemy creatures killed, as against monsters, and 500 more for the defeated hero. Both the wiki and the engine give the 500; neither gives them when the enemy hero retreats or surrenders, which needs the AI (8.3). The beaten hero leaves the map and is in the taverns' pool again (4.5.5).
  - The results window names the enemy hero as the defender, with the texts the battles already use ("A glorious victory!", "For valor in combat, …").
  - Losing or retreating: the player's hero leaves the map as against monsters, and the enemy hero keeps what is left of its army.
  - Left for later: the enemy hero casting spells and retreating (AI, 8.3 and 5.7b); its experience when it wins and the player's hero's artifacts going to it (8.1.9); the beaten hero's artifacts (8.1.8); Surrender (8.1.10).
- **An enemy hero's Resistance** (checked for 8.1.7b against the wiki's Resistance and Magic resistance pages, the engine's skills config, spell flags and spell casting, and the wiki's `GENRLTXT.TXT`):
  - The skill gives the hero's troops a 5 / 10 / 20% chance (Basic / Advanced / Expert) to resist a hostile spell. It is a chance, not an immunity: the stack can still be targeted.
  - Only hostile spells are resisted: every damage spell, and Curse, Weakness, Disrupting Ray, Slow, Misfortune, Sorrow, Blind, Forgetfulness, Berserk and Hypnotize. Helpful spells and Dispel are never resisted.
  - Each stack the spell reaches rolls on its own: a single target, every stack in an area (Fireball, Frost Ring, Inferno, Meteor Shower), every stack an Expert spell goes on (Slow, Curse), every stack Armageddon or Death Ripple hits, and every stack Chain Lightning jumps to. A stack that resists takes nothing from the spell; the others take it as usual. Chain Lightning still jumps on from a stack that resisted, its damage halved at each jump as before (the engine takes the resisting stacks out after the targets are chosen).
  - The spell is cast all the same: its spell points are spent and the hero can't cast again that round, even when every stack resisted.
  - The roll is the troops', whoever casts: the engine rolls every stack with magic resistance, so the player's own hero's Resistance lets its own stacks resist the player's own hostile area spells (Fireball, Armageddon). The wiki says only "hostile spell effects".
  - The resisting stack shows the resistance effect in place of the spell's; the engine shows it with a sound and no combat log line. The game has the words "resist" and "resists" (`GENRLTXT.TXT` 143, 144), but no source gives the line they make.
  - The Orb of Vulnerability negates it, and the Garniture of Interference, Surcoat of Counterpoise and Boots of Polarity add to it without the skill (the wiki): none of those artifacts is in the game yet (7.8). Creatures with their own magic resistance (Dwarves, Unicorns' aura) come with the creatures (6.5).
- **The beaten hero's artifacts** (checked for 8.1.8 against the wiki's Artifact page and its talk page, its Spell book and Grail pages, its `GENRLTXT.TXT`, and an open-source engine's battle results and results window):
  - Winning a battle against a hero gives the winner every artifact the beaten hero wore and carried: the worn ones first, in slot order (helmet, cape, necklace, weapon, shield, torso, right ring, left ring, feet, misc 1 to 5), then the backpack in its order. Each goes to the winner's first free slot of its kind, else to the end of the backpack, as picking one up does.
  - A worn combination artifact goes as a whole, like any other artifact.
  - The spell book is never taken (the wiki: "nor can it be lost after a defeat"); here it is not an artifact, so nothing is needed. The catapult and the war machines stay too (the engine), and the Grail is lost (the wiki): none of them is in the game yet.
  - A full backpack: an artifact with no free slot of its kind and no room in the backpack "will just disappear" (the wiki). The talk page says the slot order above decides which ones still find a slot, as the engine's order does.
  - The message, once the results window closes: "You have captured an enemy artifact!" (`GENRLTXT.TXT` 30, checked against the wiki's copy and the engine), with each captured artifact's picture. The game has only this one text; the engine's plural text is its own, so we use the game's whatever the count. No artifacts captured, no message.
  - Only a hero beaten in battle loses them: one who retreats or surrenders keeps them, which needs the AI (8.3). The player's hero losing to an enemy hero is 8.1.9.
- **Losing to an enemy hero** (checked for 8.1.9 against the wiki's Experience and Artifact pages and an open-source engine's battle results):
  - The enemy hero gets one experience point per hit point of the player's creatures killed, the same count the player's hero gets for a win. Clones don't count (the wiki).
  - Beaten: the enemy hero gets 500 more for the defeated hero, and every artifact the player's hero wore and carried, by the rule of 8.1.8 (worn ones in slot order, then the backpack; what finds no room is lost). Both sources agree.
  - Retreated: the enemy hero gets only the experience for the creatures killed, no 500 and no artifacts ("A hero from whom the enemy hero escaped receives experience for the killed enemy creatures, but does not gain experience for the defeated hero", the wiki; the engine gives the 500 and the artifacts only for a normal win). The player's hero leaves the map with its artifacts; hiring it again still makes a new hero without them (4.5.5), so they're lost to the player either way until a retreated hero waits in the tavern with them.
  - Nobody wins when a spell kills the last stacks of both sides: no experience and no artifacts for the enemy hero (the engine gives neither in a draw, nor artifacts to a winner with no units left).
  - The enemy hero levels up as any hero does: the class's primary skill roll and two secondary skill offers. The player isn't asked; the AI chooses (the engine asks the hero's owner). Until the AI (8.3), the hero takes one of the two offers at random, saved as the skill it learned (agreed for 8.1.9).
  - A combination the captured artifacts complete is assembled straight away for the enemy hero, where the player would be asked (agreed for 8.1.9; the AI decides once it plays).
  - The player sees none of it: the results window says only "Your forces suffer a bitter defeat, and … abandons your cause." or "The cowardly … flees from battle.", as now, and no message or level-up shows. It shows later, in the enemy hero's next battle (its skills and artifacts) or when it is beaten (its artifacts are captured back).
  - The Learning skill and Scholar-like bonuses to experience aren't in the game yet.

- **Surrender** (checked for 8.1.10 against the wiki's Combat, Diplomacy and Experience pages, its `GENRLTXT.TXT`, and an open-source engine's battle window, surrender cost, surrender action and battle results):
  - Only in a battle against a hero: monsters, guards and a garrison with no hero keep Surrender disabled with their tooltips. A hero defending a town can't surrender (the wiki), which only matters once the player defends (8.3).
  - The price is half the recruitment cost in gold of the creatures the hero has left on the battlefield, rounded down; other resources in a creature's cost don't count. Clones are not paid for (they aren't in the army). Diplomacy takes 20 / 40 / 60% off (Basic / Advanced / Expert), after the halving, rounded down again. The Ambassador's Sash, Diplomat's Ring and Statesman's Medal take more off, but none of them is in the game yet (7.8).
  - Clicking Surrender (key S, as in the engine) when the hero may act shows the offer: `GENRLTXT.TXT` 32, "%s states:  "I will accept your surrender and grant you and your troops safe passage for the price of %d gold."", with the enemy hero's name and the price, and OK / Cancel. Cancel goes back to the battle.
  - OK without enough gold shows "You don't have enough gold!" (`GENRLTXT.TXT` 29) and the battle goes on; the engine does this, the wiki says only that surrendering is then impossible.
  - OK with enough gold: the gold is paid, the battle ends, and the results window shows the player Defeated with "%s surrenders to the enemy, and departs in shame." (`GENRLTXT.TXT` 306; the engine shows 309, "Your forces surrender to the enemy, and depart in shame.", but our results window already names the hero in 307 and 308).
  - The enemy hero gets the experience for the creatures it killed and nothing more: no 500 and no artifacts, as after a retreat (the wiki's Experience page; the engine gives the 500 only for a normal win). The hero keeps its artifacts.
  - The hero leaves the map, as after a retreat. In the game it waits in the player's tavern with the army it had left and all its artifacts (the wiki's Combat page, the engine's tavern slots); our tavern makes a new hero on every hire (4.5.5), so that waits for its own part.
  - The enemy may refuse a surrender (the wiki, not in Horn of the Abyss): that is the AI's choice (8.3); until then every offer is accepted.
  - Who gets the gold: the wiki says the player pays the enemy player; the engine only takes it from the player. Open question for 8.1.10.
- **Fog**: each player's heroes and towns reveal the map for that player only. Already built (`game/state/fog.js`).

## Decisions

- **The players are the colours that own something in the scenario**, in colour order. Red is always the human player until hot-seat (8.2). The scenario gets no separate players list until it needs one (faction or AI settings, 8.3 or 9.1).
- **Every hero gets a flag in its owner's colour**, the player's own too, as towns, mines and dwellings have.
- **A garrisoned town is fought on an open field until sieges (5.10)**, even when it has walls.
- **The hero walks up to an enemy hero before heroes can attack** (the player's choice): the swords show, the hero stops on the tile next to it and nothing happens there until 8.1.7. Meeting a hero only opens the exchange screen when both are the same player's. Flying passes over an enemy hero as over the player's own heroes, and no spell lands on its tile.
- **The helmet cursor is only for the player's own heroes.** Over an enemy hero the hero can't reach it is the arrow, with a town selected too.
- **An enemy hero's popup keeps the hero box's skill icons** with their values empty, so it has the same shape as the player's own (agreed for 8.1.3).
- Other players' resources start empty. Starting resources and income for them come with each player's day (8.1.13).

## Built

- **8.1.1**: every hero, town, mine and dwelling a player owns carries that player's flag on the map, and the minimap shows them in that colour on the tiles the player has explored. The kingdom overview lists the other players' flags, in colour order, under Enemies. The demo map has a blue player in the south-east corner: Kirkwall, Sir Mullich and the Sulfur Dune.
- **8.1.2**: an enemy hero blocks its tile and can't be selected or met. The swords show over one the hero can reach and the hero walks up next to it, where nothing happens yet; the arrow shows over one it can't reach.
- **8.1.3**: right-clicking or long-pressing another player's hero shows its portrait, name and army, each stack with its size word under it ("Pack"), and the skill icons without values; no morale, luck or spell points.
- **8.1.4 and 8.1.5**, in one PR: entering another player's mine, dwelling or undefended town takes it for the player, through the same rule as a neutral one (the visitor's owner differs from the object's), so no new code was needed; the tests pin it. The flag turns red on the map and the minimap, the popup says "(Red)", the mine message shows, the income and the dwelling's growth bonus go to the player, a dwelling offers its creatures, and another player's guarded dwelling is fought for first. The demo map has a blue Archers' Tower beside Kirkwall.
- **8.1.6**: entering another player's town, or a neutral one, whose garrison has creatures and no hero in it starts a battle against the garrison, fought on an open field; the swords show over it. Winning empties the garrison, takes the town and opens its town screen, with 500 more experience when the town has a Fort or better. Losing or retreating leaves the town to its owner with what is left of its garrison, and Surrender says there is no hero to surrender to. A scenario may now give any town a garrison; the demo map gives Kirkwall 15 Pikemen and 8 Archers.
- **8.1.7**: walking onto another player's hero starts a battle against its army, with its Attack, Defense, morale, luck and artifacts' immunities; an artifact that cancels morale or luck does so for both sides whichever hero wears it. The enemy hero stands on the right of the battlefield with its flag. Winning gives the hero 500 more experience and takes the beaten hero off the map and the minimap, and the taverns can offer it again. Losing or retreating leaves the enemy hero with what is left of its army. Surrender stays disabled until 8.1.10.
- **8.1.7b**: a hero's Resistance gives each of its stacks a 5 / 10 / 20% chance to resist each hostile spell the player casts, rolled per stack; an enemy hero's army resists the player's spells and the player's own stacks resist the player's own area spells (agreed for 8.1.7b). A stack that resisted takes nothing, shows a shield ring where the spell would have burst, and the log says "The Pikemen resist the Magic Arrow." ("The Pikeman resists …"), our own wording around the game's words "resist" and "resists" (agreed for 8.1.7b). The roll is saved with the cast. The demo map gives Sir Mullich Expert Resistance.
- **8.1.8**: beating another player's hero gives the hero every artifact it wore and carried, worn ones first in slot order, then its backpack, each in the first free slot of its kind or at the end of the backpack; a worn combination goes as a whole, and what finds no room is lost. When the results window closes, "You have captured an enemy artifact!" shows the captured artifacts, eight to a window as Scholar's message does (agreed for 8.1.8), then the hero is asked once about each combination they complete (agreed for 8.1.8). Saved as `fact.hero.artifacts.captured`. The demo map gives Sir Mullich the Helm of Chaos, the Blackshard of the Dead Knight and a Skull Helmet in his backpack.
- **8.1.9**: an enemy hero that beats the player's hero gets one experience point per hit point of the player's creatures killed and 500 more, and every artifact the hero wore and carried, as the player's hero gets them in 8.1.8; a combination they complete is assembled at once. After a retreat it gets only the experience for the creatures killed. It levels up without asking the player, taking one of the two skill offers at random. The player sees no message: the results window is the one battles already show. With no stacks left (a spell killed the last of both sides) it gets nothing.
- Left for the next parts: the enemy hero's details through Visions (8.1.16), and a town of another player names no owner in its popup until the town box (8.1.15).
