# AI basics

Roadmap 8.3. Computer players take their turns: the player watches the hourglass while each one plays, and sees its heroes walk wherever the player's fog of war doesn't hide them. What the AI decides is kept to one thing here, walking to resource piles and picking them up; the other decisions (exploring, mines, towns, recruiting, fighting, defending) are 8.4. Builds on 8.1.12 (`docs/notes/2026-11-10-player-system.md`), which made each computer player's turn an empty one in colour order.

## Parts

One part, one PR:

1. **8.3.1 Computer players play their turns.** After End Turn each computer player plays in colour order: the info box shows the hourglass with that player's crest, the minimap is covered by the shield, and the player's clicks and keys on the adventure map do nothing. Each of its heroes walks to the nearest resource pile it can reach without a fight and picks it up, again and again until it has no movement points left or nothing to go to; a pile too far for today is walked towards. Moves the player can see are walked at the player's own speed with the map following the hero; moves under the fog happen at once. Then the new day starts as before. A reload during a computer player's turn carries on with that turn.

Resource collection, listed under 8.4, comes here as the AI's first decision: it needs no question answered and no battle, so it shows the loop working.

## Rules, as checked

- **The info box during another player's turn** shows that player's crest and an hourglass whose sand runs. Checked against an open-source engine's info bar (`startEnemyTurn`: the crest by player colour, the sand and glass animations) and our own roadmap item (8.1 moved it here). The original game's hourglass is a sprite we don't have: drawn in our own style.
- **The minimap during a computer player's turn** is covered by a shield picture (the engine's `AIShield`, shown while a computer player plays, not during a human player's turn in hot-seat). Only the engine says so; a web search found nothing either way.
- **No input during a computer player's turn**: the engine disables both the side panel and the map view while a computer player plays (the shortcuts all need the player to be making their turn). So no clicks on the map, no buttons, no hero or town list, no keyboard shortcuts.
- **Enemy moves on screen**: a step is shown when its start or end tile is visible to the player (not under their fog); the map follows the moving hero while it is shown. A step neither end of which the player can see happens at once. The engine's default enemy move time equals the player's hero move time. Checked against the engine's map view controller and a bug report of the engine, which lists System Options' Enemy Move Speed with three speeds that show enemy moves and a fourth that hides them. The speed setting itself is left for later (System Options).
- **Picking up a resource** gives it to the hero's owner, with no message; the pile disappears as it does for the player's hero when the player sees it.
- **The enemy turn music** ("enemy-turn" in the engine) is left for music per context (10.5).

## Decisions

Proposed, waiting for approval:

- **The AI's choice**: the nearest resource pile by the movement points it costs, ties broken by the pile's tile (top row first, then left), so the choice needs no roll and replays the same. A pile is a target only when the hero can reach it without stepping next to a monster or onto anything else on the way (a battle, a question or a visit is 8.4's).
- **A hero standing in its town stays there** until 8.4 decides when a defender may leave (threat response); otherwise Edric would leave Kirkwall open on the demo map.
- **No minimum length for a computer player's turn**: a turn with nothing to show passes at once, as 8.1.12's do now.
- **Where the map is when the player's turn starts**: centred on the player's selected hero, as a new day already does.
- The roadmap's "AI is thinking..." indicator is dropped: the hourglass is the game's own sign.
